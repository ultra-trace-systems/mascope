Any assignment can be verified by hand: **confirm** it, **reject** it, or mark
it **unsure**, with an optional note. Confirming also asks for the level of
evidence behind the judgment &mdash; from a reference standard, MS/MS or an
orthogonal technique down to the isotope/adduct pattern or a visual check.
Rejecting is a first-class answer, not a lesser one: confirmed and rejected
assignments together form the labelled record that future confidence
calibration is fit on. Verdicts attach to the peak, formula and ionization
mechanism rather than to one run, so they carry over when the sample is
re-assigned. One verdict covers a whole compound: judging it from any of its
isotopologue peaks records a single verdict, shown on all of them.
