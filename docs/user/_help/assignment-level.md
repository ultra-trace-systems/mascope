The **identification level** says what you may state of a peak's formula, in
the scale the field uses (Schymanski et al. 2014): a number from 5 (a
measured mass) to 1 (a confirmed structure), with a letter for the kind of
evidence behind it. Mascope states the formula levels from what each run
measured:

- **5b** &mdash; a mass, and the reading is refuted: a line of its isotope
  pattern the spectrum should show is absent, no molecule has the formula, or
  the instrument did not separate the peak from a neighbour, so it could not be
  tested.
- **5a** &mdash; a mass with rivals: another formula on the peak's shortlist
  that no test excluded, listed. A peak whose shortlist was not measured reads
  5a too, with *rivals not measured*.
- **4b** &mdash; the ion's formula: every rival excluded, but the ion also
  reads as another molecule with another adduct, and nothing settled which.
- **4a** &mdash; the neutral formula: every rival excluded, and the split into
  molecule and adduct pinned.

On a peak at 4a, the run's chemistry data can say more:

- **3b** &mdash; one functional group: the adduct the peak was read through
  says a class of molecule, such as an acid deprotonated by a nitrate
  reagent. The row names the class, *an acid of this formula*, and no
  compound.
- **2b** &mdash; one plausible structure: a cited reference list marks the
  formula as having only one, as trifluoroacetic acid's. The row names the
  structure, the list and the work it cites.

The chip takes a colour of its own per number, apart from the tier's: slate
for 5 (a mass), blue for 4 (a formula), plum from 3 up (a claim about
structure), always with its text. Beside it, **why** says what earned the level and **would lift it**
what test is missing. **Rivals** lists each other formula with the test that
put it out &mdash; by mass, by isotopes or by a second channel &mdash; or
*open*. An isotope line shows its main peak's level. The level never changes
the tier.

The batch states a level of its own too, from checks that read every sample
at once. A bromine or chlorine line found at the exact spacing across the
batch, or a sulfur or silicon line the batch found, puts out the rivals that
cannot make it: a row with no rival left open then reads 4a or 4b. The 14N
twin of a labelled ion, found across the batch, pins its split: 4b to 4a. A
peak the batch reads as another peak's 37Cl or 81Br line reads 5b, where its
own formula holds no such atom. The batch ledger's **Level** column shows the
batch's level. On a sample's row, it shows under the row's own, marked **the
batch's**, where it differs. The batch never lowers a 3b or 2b a run stated.
A sample with no run of its own shows its batch peak's level, marked with the
batch's **#**, as a batch-level verdict is.

Where a verdict you or a colleague recorded states a level, the chip and the
**Level** column show that level instead, and the hover says *claim*; with
none, it says *engine*. A batch-level verdict's level wears the **#** too. A
claim recorded before the letters shows its number alone.
