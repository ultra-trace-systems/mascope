The **identification level** says what you may state of a peak's formula, in
the scale the field uses (Schymanski et al. 2014): a number from 5 (a
measured mass) to 1 (a confirmed structure), with a letter for the kind of
evidence behind it. Mascope states the formula levels from what each run
measured:

- **5b** &mdash; a mass, and the reading is refuted: a line of its isotope
  pattern the spectrum should show is absent, no molecule has the formula, or
  the instrument did not separate the peak from a neighbour, so it could not be
  tested.
- **5a** &mdash; a mass with rivals: another formula on the peak's shortlist
  that no test excluded, listed. A peak whose shortlist was not measured reads
  5a too, with *rivals not measured*.
- **4b** &mdash; the ion's formula: every rival excluded, but the ion also
  reads as another molecule with another adduct, and nothing settled which.
- **4a** &mdash; the neutral formula: every rival excluded, and the split into
  molecule and adduct pinned.

The chip takes a colour of its own per number, apart from the tier's: slate
for 5 (a mass), blue for 4 (a formula), plum from 3 up (a claim about
structure), always with its text. Beside it, **why** says what earned the level and **would lift it**
what test is missing. **Rivals** lists each other formula with the test that
put it out &mdash; by mass, by isotopes or by a second channel &mdash; or
*open*. An isotope line shows its main peak's level. The level never changes
the tier.
