- **Peak assignment reads "the same ion", "a second channel" and "the
  sample shows this molecule" one way everywhere.** Two list compounds that
  make one ion through two channels are one candidate on their peak, not two
  tied rivals, and a target library's rows show the other readings of their
  ion as a reference list's do. Such a row's identification level is then
  4a, its split pinned by the library, where it read 4b when its ion could
  hold another channel's reagent unit. A reading through a secondary channel
  is held at *candidate* by one rule, read once after the partner gate has
  settled the readings: a partner is the same molecule committed at
  *candidate* or better through one of the mode's own channels, from either
  stage, where the search's rows used to count any tier of their own stage
  alone. The fragment claim reads what the sample shows after the mass
  gate's check, as the other passes do. Tiers can move on samples with
  secondary channels.
