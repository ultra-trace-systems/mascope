- **The peak inspector answers first.** The card opens with the answer - the listed name
  and its list, the formula, the ion line (mechanism, ion, m/z, line) and one row of
  chips for the tier, the level and the verdict - then a **Why** box titled for the tier.
  The box draws the row's evidence against the run's own tier bands, gives the product
  of fit and plausibility, and lists the reasons in one line each, those that hold the
  tier back first, with the number each turned on; a click shows the run's sentence. A
  second channel or a series lists the batch's rows it spans, each m/z a link to its
  peak. Four tiles say the mass error, the isotope lines found, the confidence (a tie
  written out) and the batch. The isotope pattern (now a stick plot over a list that no
  longer scrolls sideways), the alternatives (with *Use* always shown), the batch's
  readings and the identification level fold under one line each, and the verdict bar
  stays at the card's foot. A change by hand reads as one line with its action
  (*Undo*, *Release*), its full account on hover. *Find more* is now *Find other
  formulas for this peak*, and the search pane carries the same title.
