- **Peak assignment reads each formula-shape rule from one table, and only a
  reference list spares a row a cap on its composition.** No element ratio
  rejects a formula any more: the composition search now proposes sulfur
  formulas under ten carbons, chlorine and bromine formulas under twenty
  wherever its element grid holds them, and formulas with no carbon at all,
  which the carbon-free cap holds at candidate. The chemistry context's ratio
  windows weigh a formula down instead of rejecting it, and do not judge a
  compound a reference list names. A compound only your target library names
  is now capped like any other row, and still shows its match; a radical is
  capped whoever names it. Runs record tiering rules version 17. See "Why a
  row holds its tier" in `docs/user/how-it-works/peak-assignment.md`.

- **The bromide and iodide profiles no longer search chlorine or bromine.**
  Their element grids held them, and read the reagent's own ion as a
  halogenated molecule: a molecule seen through `[M+Br]-` has the mass of that
  molecule with HBr added, seen through `[M-H]-`. A halogenated compound in
  those runs is read from a list, or from its chlorine twin line on a bromide
  source.
